How We Approach It

The first decision is native versus cross-platform, and it genuinely depends on the app: native (Swift/SwiftUI, Kotlin/Jetpack Compose) when you need day-one access to platform features or a performance ceiling nothing else touches, cross-platform (React Native, Flutter, or Kotlin Multiplatform) when shared logic and faster delivery across both platforms matters more.

Whichever path fits, the backend integration gets designed early, not bolted on at the end. Authentication, offline data sync, and push notifications are the plumbing that determines whether an app feels reliable, and they're much harder to retrofit than to design in from the start.

We build for the app store review process as part of development, not as a surprise at submission, so metadata, screenshots, and store-specific requirements get handled before you're racing a deadline.

Common Questions

Native or cross-platform, which is right for us? expand_more

It depends on your priorities. If you need the absolute best performance or day-one access to new OS features, native wins. If you want to ship to both platforms faster with one codebase, cross-platform is usually the better call. We'll walk through the trade-offs for your specific app.

Can the app work offline? expand_more

Yes, if that's a requirement we design for it. Offline-first architecture with local data sync is something we build in from the start rather than adding after the fact.

Do you handle App Store and Play Store submission? expand_more

Yes, including the metadata, screenshots, and review requirements for both stores, because that's part of the delivery, not a separate task left for you to handle.
